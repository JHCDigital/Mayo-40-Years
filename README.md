# Introducing the Speakers
## Dr. Virginia Wright-Peterson
Dr. Virginia Wright-Peterson is a Minnesotan author, educator and speaker. Her books explore themes of intersectionality, history and resilience, with a special focus on women's history and the history of Mayo Clinic. This program celebrated the publication of her fourth book, "Mayo Clinic in Florida: A Legacy of Dedication, Innovation, and Excellence," which was released in tandem with Mayo Clinic's 40th anniversary in Florida. Her three previous books were all finalists for the Minnesota Book Award. Wright-Peterson has been teaching for over fifteen years at schools like the University of Minnesota Rochester and Rochester Community and Technical College. She was also a Fullbright Scholar who taught at the University of Algeria. Additionally, she worked alongside the American Red Cross on a U.S. Military base in Iraq. Wright-Peterson continues to support and engage with the history and writing communities through attending events and offering consultations. 
## Dr. James Bolling
Dr. James Bolling has certifications in ophthamology, retinal diseases, and intraocular tumors. He began his journey as part of the staff of Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Minnesota, in 1986. He transferred to Mayo Clinic Florida as one of the original physicians to staff the Jacksonville location. Alongside his experience with Mayo Clinic in Florida, he also worked for 30 years as a consultant at Nemours Children's Clinic. He served as chair of the department of ophthalmology and the Mayo Clinic Florida Board of Governors. Dr. Bolling retired from clinical practice in 2021 and is a senior research fellow at Mayo Clinic. In his current position he assists with research and administrative activities on a part-time basis.

The narrative covers Mayo Clinic in Florida from its conception through its 40 years of service and expansion. The book sought out to answer an ambitious question: Could Mayo Clinic's distinctive model of care be extended to serve patients in another part of the country? Before the construction of its Jacksonville location, the only Mayo campus at the time was located in Rochester, Minnesota. The introduction of major regional campus was uncharted territory. Wright-Peterson credits Dr. Steven Buskirk and Dr. Kent Thielen for the inspiration to write the book. Dr. Buskirk is Professor of Radiation Oncology and Chair of the Department of Radiation Oncology at Mayo Clinic in Florida. Dr. Thielen is the vice president of Mayo Clinic and CEO of Mayo Clinic in Florida. As Wright-Peterson dug deeper into the story she used interviews conducted by the Historical Activities Committee and archival material made accessible by the efforts of archivist Carole LaRochelle and the Senior Division Chair and Education Administrator, Nell Robinson. The Historical Activities Committee interviewed over one hundred people who were involved in the development of Mayo Clinic. These stories allowed Wright-Peterson to weave a complete history centered on the authentic journey of the clinic and those involved with its development. Ultimately, the book highlights how Mayo Clinic Florida honors its legacy by preserving the stories and values of those who championed its success.

# The History
## Expansion to the South
Mayo Clinic in Rochester had done very well for itself, operating exclusively out of Minnesota for 122 years. It is still globally recognized as a leading medical facility. J.E. and Flo Davis, owners of the Winn-Dixie grocery store chain, along with Dr. Frances Bartlett Kinne, president of Jacksonville University, are largely recognized as trailblazers for the existence of Mayo Clinic in Florida. J.E. Davis had been well acquainted with Mayo Clinic in Rochester and had been travelling to and from the facility for years. There were clear benefits for Mayo to expand to the South such as the warm weather and the wider area of impact. In 1978, J.E. was determined to bring the successful clinic down to Florida after hearing that his doctor, Dr. Hugh Butt, had developed frost bite on his commute to the hospital. He began a campaign to solicit community support in Jacksonville, Florida, and shake Mayo leadership's apprehension of the idea. In 1982, the Davis family offered to donate 140 acres of land and successfully rose 6 million dollars by 1984. At the same time, Mayo CEO Dr. Gene Mayberry gathered a team of four physicians and one administrator to analyze how the expansion proposal could actually go. These meetings were held as spaghetti dinners at the Hardwick Building in Rochester. On August 20, 1984, Mayo Clinic opened a "satellite" clinic in Jacksonville, Florida, with no intention of opening a full hospital. However, the success from that little test clinic was undeniable and Mayo was convinced that they needed to go all in for the expansion. 

## Mayo Clinic in Florida
On October 3, 1986, Mayo Clinic held its dedication ceremony for the completed Davis Building. 800 guests attended the celebration, including J.E. Davis, Jacksonville Mayor Jake Godbold, and the Reverend Billy Graham, who was the speaker of the event. The building was named in honor of J.E. Davis and his commitment to bringing Mayo Clinic to Florida. Rev. Billy Graham recollected how far the history of Mayo had come from Dr. William Worrall Mayo and his medical practice to their official second location. The Davis building was five stories tall and cost approximately $35 million. Ten days after the celebration, on October 13, 1986, Mayo Clinic in Florida officially opened their doors to patients. The hospital had accumulated over 2,700 appointments with patients coming from 30 states and six foreign countries. Approximately 35 physicians and 145 allied health staff stood ready to see if they could replicate the high quality care that the clinic in Rochester was known for. The clinic began growing far beyond what its founders had initially projected, swiftly becoming the most popular hospital in Jacksonville. In the 1990s, the Davis Building expanded by four floors, and new research facilities, outpatient surgery services, and radiation oncology centers were added. Scientists studied diseases like cancer, alzheimer's disease, and cardiovascular conditions as the research department of the Florida campus grew. Today, Mayo Clinic in Florida is a well respected medical center that stands amongst the best statewide. What began as an idea from J.E. Davis and a small group of determined community members has developed into one of the most important healthcare institutions in Northeast Florida.

# The Exhibit
This program's physical exhibit was curated by the Jacksonville History Center's research archivist, Jason Robilio. The focus of the exhibit was on medical history throughout Jacksonville with special attention to the St. Luke's Hospital. The Jacksonville History Center archives has ample material from Old St. Luke's because its main building resides in the St. Luke's Hospital that was built in 1878. The hospital was well established and deeply significant to Jacksonville long before Mayo came to town. However, when the Mayo Clinic in Jacksonville opened, Mayo purchased St. Luke’s Hospital in 1987 to serve as its primary admitting hospital. After their affiliation for 21 years, St. Luke's was passed onto St. Vincent's Health System.
