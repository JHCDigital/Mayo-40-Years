# Introducing the Speaker
## Virginia Wright-Peterson
Virginia Wright-Peterson is a Minnesotan author, educator and speaker. Her books explore themes of intersectionality, history and resilience, with a special focus on women's history and the history of Mayo Clinic. This program celebrated the publication of her fourth book, "Mayo Clinic in Florida: A Legacy of Dedication, Innovation, and Excellence," which was released in tandem with Mayo Clinic's 40th anniversary in Florida. Her three previous books were all finalists for the Minnesota Book Award. Wright-Peterson has been teaching for over fifteen years at schools like the University of Minnesota Rochester and Rochester Community and Technical College. She was also a Fullbright Scholar who taught at the University of Algeria. Additionally, she worked alongside the American Red Cross on a U.S. Military base in Iraq. Wright-Peterson continues to support and engage with the history and writing communities through attending events and offering consultations. 

 # Mayo Clinic in Florida: A Legacy of Dedication, Innovation, and Excellence
The narrative covers Mayo Clinic in Florida from its conception through its 40 years of service and expansion. The book sought out to answer an ambitious question: Could Mayo Clinic's distinctive model of care be extended to serve patients in another part of the country? Before the birth of its Jacksonville location, the singular primary Mayo campus at the time was located in Rochester, Minnesota. So, the introduction of major regional campus was uncharted territory. Wright-Peterson credits Dr. Steven Buskirk and Dr. Kent Thielen for the inspiration to write the book. Dr. Buskirk is Professor of Radiation Oncology and Chair of the Department of Radiation Oncology at Mayo Clinic in Florida. Dr. Thielen is the vice president of Mayo Clinic, and CEO of Mayo Clinic in Florida. As Wright-Peterson dug deeper into the story she culminated resources from interviews the Historical Activities Committee conducted and archival material that was made accessible by the efforts of archivist Carole LaRochelle and Senior Division Chair and Education Administrator Nell Robinson. The Historical Activities Committee interviewed over one hundred people who were involved in the development of Mayo Clinic. These stories allowed Wright-Peterson to weave a complete history centered on the authentic journey of the clinic and those involved with its development. Ultimately, the book highlights how Mayo Clinic Florida honors its legacy by preserving the stories and values of those who championed its success.

# The History
## Expansion to the South
Mayo Clinic in Rochester had done very well for itself, operating exclusively out of Minnesota for 122 years. It is still globally recognized as a leading medical facility. J.E. and Flo Davis, owners of the Winn-Dixie grocery store chain along with Dr. Frances Bartlett Kinne, president of Jacksonville University are largely recognized as trailblazers for Mayo Clinic in Florida's existence. J.E. Davis had been well acquainted with Mayo Clinic in Rochester and had been travelling to and from the facility for years. There were clear benefits for Mayo to expand to the South including the weather and creating a widespread area of impact. In 1978, J.E. was determined to bring the successful clinic down to Florida after hearing that his doctor, Dr. Hugh Butt, had developed frost bite on his commute to the hospital. He began a campaign to solicit community support in Jacksonville, Florida, and shake Mayo leadership's apprehension of the idea. In 1982, the Davis family offered to donate 140 acres of land and raise 6 million dollars, which they were successful in by 1984. At the same time, Mayo CEO, Dr. Gene Mayberry gathered a team of four physicians and one administrator to analyze how the expansion proposal could actually go. These meetings were held as spaghetti dinners at the Hardwick Building in Rochester. On August 20, 1984, Mayo Clinic opened a "satellite" clinic in Jacksonville, Florida.
